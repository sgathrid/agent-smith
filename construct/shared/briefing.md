# Briefing

How the top-level session hands work to a helper and takes it back.

## Brief

Every handoff is a written brief a newcomer could act on. It holds:
- The goal and why it matters.
- The facts that bear on it: paths, symbols, what you already ruled out.
- Scope: read-only, or exactly what it may write.
- Constraints that are real. Leave out preferences dressed as rules.
- The shape of the deliverable, in a sentence.
- The evidence each claim needs (file:line, a command and its output, a reproduction).
- "No findings is a valid answer." Without this line, helpers invent findings.
- "Do the work yourself; start no helpers of your own." Only the top-level session delegates.

Skip checklists and option menus. A helper with a clear goal judges better than one following steps.

## Scale

Answer inline first. Use one helper when the work is large enough to flood your context or a fresh view matters. Use several only when stakes or uncertainty justify the cost.
Weigh four things: stakes (what breaks if this is wrong), uncertainty (how far apart plausible answers are), reversibility (how cheap a wrong call is to undo) and breadth (how many independent slices exist).
Before fanning out across many units, pilot one unit and read its result. A bad brief copied ten times costs ten times as much.

## Diversity

Independent views are worth more when they come from different model families, since one family tends to share its blind spots. Use whatever workers are available; a single family is fine when that is what exists.
The judge should not be the author. Work gets its verdict from a helper, or a session, that did not write it.

## Reports

A report is a claim. Check each load-bearing claim against the code before you build on it.
Drop results that arrive without the evidence the brief asked for.
When a helper fails, retry once with the failure named in the new brief. If it fails again, report the item as open with what you know.
